Understanding EMDR

A structured approach to trauma therapy.

EMDR stands for Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing. It is a structured psychotherapy approach that may help people process distressing experiences. During parts of EMDR, you may focus on an aspect of an experience while engaging in guided bilateral stimulation, such as eye movements or alternating taps.

01

Start by understanding what brings you in

Erin listens first and asks simple questions to make sure she understands. You do not need to begin by sharing every detail of what happened.

02

Decide whether EMDR fits

EMDR is not an automatic starting point or a requirement for working with Erin. You and Erin can consider your current needs, comfort, goals, and the online format before deciding how to proceed.

03

Prepare before processing

EMDR includes preparation. Erin can explain the process, help identify supports and coping resources, and answer questions before any work with a distressing memory.

04

Keep your goals open to change

You choose the goals of therapy and can change them as your needs become clearer. Erin checks in throughout the work rather than assuming one method or pace fits everyone.

Online EMDR sessions

Specialized trauma care without traveling to an office.

Erin provides EMDR by telehealth to adults who are physically located in Indiana at the time of care. Online delivery can make this treatment option available when traveling to an office is difficult or when telehealth is simply a better fit.

A private setting

A reliable device, stable internet connection, and a private place to talk help support an online session. Erin can discuss whether the setting is workable for your needs.

A collaborative pace

Erin explains what to expect, listens to your feedback, and keeps the pace responsive to your comfort and readiness.

Care that respects identity

Met Needs and Wellness welcomes LGBTQ+ adults, older adults, and adults with intellectual disabilities. Trauma care remains centered on each person's lived experience, communication, and goals.

Choosing an approach

EMDR is one option within a broader plan.

Some people contact Erin because they are specifically looking for EMDR. Others know only that they feel overwhelmed, stuck, or affected by difficult experiences. You do not need to know which approach you want before reaching out.

01

Several evidence-based approaches

Erin can draw from EMDR and other evidence-based therapy approaches. The method should serve your concerns and goals rather than determine them.

02

Trauma and complex situations

Care can make room for experiences that feel layered, hard to explain, or connected with relationships, identity, grief, health, safety, or daily responsibilities.

03

No promised timeline or outcome

Therapy is individual. Erin can help you understand possible next steps, but no method guarantees a particular result or the number of sessions the work will take.
