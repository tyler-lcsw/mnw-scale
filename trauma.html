Understanding the work

Trauma care begins with listening, not assumptions.

Difficult experiences can show up as feeling on edge, shutting down, avoiding reminders, struggling in relationships, or feeling overwhelmed by ordinary demands. Erin listens first and asks simple questions to make sure she understands what matters to you.

01

Establish a workable pace

Trauma-focused work does not need to move faster than the client is ready for. Erin's approach emphasizes collaboration, clarity, and attention to what feels manageable.

02

Notice patterns in daily life

Sessions may explore how difficult experiences connect with present-day reactions, relationships, routines, beliefs, or a sense of safety.

03

Use methods with intention

Erin offers online EMDR therapy alongside other evidence-based techniques. Any approach is considered within the broader therapeutic relationship and your goals.

04

Keep real life in view

The work stays connected to daily functioning, responsibilities, support systems, and the practical changes a client hopes to make.

Complex trauma

When experiences are layered, the context matters.

Complex trauma may involve repeated, prolonged, or interconnected experiences. Erin's trauma-focused work makes room for the ways those layers can affect identity, trust, relationships, and everyday functioning without reducing you to a diagnosis.
