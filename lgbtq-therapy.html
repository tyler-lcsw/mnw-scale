Support for what is affecting you now

Affirming therapy can make room for the whole picture.

Some people seek therapy for an experience connected to identity. Others want help with trauma, feeling overwhelmed, relationships, grief, stress, or uncertainty about what comes next. Erin does not assume that identity is either the problem or the only important part of your experience.

01

Trauma and complex experiences

Difficult experiences may continue to affect safety, trust, relationships, or everyday decisions. Erin offers trauma-focused care, including online EMDR when it is an appropriate fit.

02

Identity and self-understanding

You decide whether identity is central to the conversation, one part of it, or simply something you need a therapist to respect without explanation or debate.

03

Relationships and boundaries

Therapy can explore communication, connection, boundaries, family dynamics, dating, partnership, or patterns that no longer feel useful.

04

Overwhelm and feeling stuck

You do not need a perfect label for what is happening. Care can begin with the parts of daily life that feel difficult and work toward less overwhelm and a clearer path forward.

How Erin works

Respect shows up in the conversation.

Affirming care is more than a welcoming statement. Erin uses the name, pronouns, relationship language, and identity terms you choose. She keeps the work connected to your concerns and checks that she understands before moving forward.

Listening comes first

You can start with what feels most important or most manageable to say.

Goals stay yours

You can choose the direction of therapy and change your goals as your needs or understanding change.

More than one approach

EMDR is one option within Erin’s broader evidence-based, collaborative approach to trauma and complex situations.
